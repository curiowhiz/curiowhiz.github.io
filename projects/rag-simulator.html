RAG Simulator

Retrieval-Augmented Generation gets talked about constantly in AI product discussions, but most people who have to make decisions about it (PMs, engineers new to the pattern, stakeholders sitting in a roadmap review) have never actually watched a query move through chunking, embedding, retrieval, and generation. This simulator makes that pipeline visible and interactive instead of a diagram on a slide.

Who It's For

PMs scoping their first RAG feature, engineers who understand embeddings in theory but haven't traced a request end to end, and anyone who needs to explain to a stakeholder why a retrieval-grounded answer is different from a model simply guessing. No ML background required to follow along.

What It Shows

A query goes in, and the simulator walks through the stages a real RAG system executes: splitting source documents into chunks, turning those chunks into vector embeddings, retrieving the passages closest to the query, and assembling the retrieved context into the prompt an LLM ultimately generates its answer from.

Why It Exists

Most RAG explanations are either too abstract (a box-and-arrow diagram) or too deep (raw embedding math). This sits in between: concrete enough to build real intuition for how chunk size and retrieval quality change an answer, without requiring anyone to read vector-similarity code to get there.

How To Use It

Open the simulator, enter a query, and step through each stage of the pipeline as it runs. Try varying the query to see how retrieval results shift, and compare that against what the final generated answer looks like, which is the fastest way to build a feel for where a RAG system can go right or wrong.

Try It Yourself

Run a query through the full retrieval pipeline and watch each stage happen live, with no setup or account required.
