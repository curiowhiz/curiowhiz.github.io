Vendor ITC Recovery Agent

Under India's GST regime, a business can only claim back the tax it paid a vendor if that vendor files the matching invoice correctly; a vendor filing late silently blocks real money. This tool catches that gap automatically and automates the vendor follow-up needed to recover it, with a human confirming every email before it sends.

The Problem

Input Tax Credit (ITC) is the GST a business pays a vendor, claimable back against the GST it collects from its own customers. It can only be claimed if the invoice shows up correctly in GSTR-2B, the government's monthly statement of what vendors reported selling. A late filing, an omitted invoice, or a wrong amount silently blocks credit the business already earned, and most SMEs have no easy way to catch it every filing cycle.

The Approach

A Next.js app on Vercel handles upload and the dashboard; Supabase (Postgres, Auth, Storage, pgvector) is the system of record; Claude is called in only for the judgment calls a deterministic check can't resolve: fuzzy-match arbitration, bucket classification, and email drafting; Brevo delivers the vendor email once a human approves it.

Explainability

Every dashboard card has a "Why this bucket?" panel that retrieves from a small, curated GST-rules knowledge base and explains the classification grounded strictly in what it finds. If the knowledge base doesn't cover a situation, it says so plainly instead of guessing at tax law.

Want to dive deeper?

  • Ingest: A CSV/XLSX purchase register or GSTR-2B file lands in Storage; columns get auto-matched to standard fields before anything is written
  • Deterministic match, first: The system tries an exact key match (GSTIN, invoice number, and amount) before it ever asks the AI anything
  • AI arbitrates only what's genuinely unclear: Near-duplicates go to Claude, which always returns a confidence score and a plain-language reason, never a bare yes or no
  • Bucket, with the RCM check first: Reverse-charge invoices are pulled out before any mismatch logic runs, so a compliant vendor is never mislabeled
  • The human is the last gate: An email exists only as a draft, with real numbers substituted in by code, until someone clicks Confirm & Send
  • Setup: businesses, consent_log
  • Ingestion: uploads, invoices_purchase_register, invoices_gstr2b
  • Reconciliation: matches, buckets
  • Record & follow-up: audit_log, messages_sent
  • audit_log is the one table almost everything writes to, and nothing overwrites: the paper trail a CA could review before filing
  • Retrieval before explanation: the panel searches a curated GST-rules library (CGST Act, GST IMS FAQs, a GSTN advisory) before Claude writes anything
  • Grounded strictly in what's retrieved: Claude explains using only the retrieved passage, never its general knowledge of tax law
  • Says "not covered" rather than guessing: if the library doesn't address a situation, the panel says so plainly; this is verified in practice, not just instructed in the prompt
  • RCM explanations are explicit: they state outright that nothing is wrong with the vendor's filing
  • Deterministic-first matching keeps the AI in the minority of cases: cheaper, faster, and easier to audit than routing every invoice through a model
  • Structured output (never a bare yes/no) on every AI call makes "why this match?" a mechanical guarantee rather than a hope
  • RCM/excluded invoices are checked before any other classification logic, because falsely flagging a compliant vendor is a real reputational risk, not just a bug
  • Every match and bucket decision is written to an append-only audit log with its rationale, the feature that makes "why this bucket?" and any future compliance review possible at all
